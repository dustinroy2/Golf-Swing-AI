# Name Options — Golf Swing AI

Five naming candidates evaluated for branding potential, App Store performance, and long-term defensibility.

---

## OnPlane

**Logo concept**
Wordmark in a clean geometric sans-serif (Inter or Helvetica Neue). The "O" in OnPlane gets the green accent (#3fb950) and optionally doubles as a side-view swing arc icon — a simple curved line through the letter suggesting swing plane. The rest of the word is white on dark. No tagline baked into the logo; the mark stands alone.

**Taglines**
1. "Get on plane. Stay on plane."
2. "Your swing, corrected in seconds."
3. "Film it. Fix it. Flush it."

**App Store subtitle** (max 30 chars)
`AI Swing Coach, Two Angles`

**Easy Mode branding**
"QuickPlane" — implies speed and simplicity while staying on-brand. In-app label: "QuickPlane Mode: two clips, instant feedback."

**One-line elevator pitch**
OnPlane uses AI pose detection to film your swing from two angles, find exactly what's wrong, and show you how to fix it — in under a minute.

**Potential issues**
- "On Plane" (two words) is an extremely common phrase in golf instruction — YouTube videos, books, coaching apps all use it. Strong organic search alignment, but no trademark protection for the phrase itself.
- Risk of blending into generic instruction content in search results ("on plane golf swing" returns 50M+ results).
- App name "OnPlane" (one word) is more defensible but could still face App Store discoverability dilution.
- No obvious trademark conflicts found for the compound word — favorable for registration.

## RightPlane

**Logo concept**
The word splits visually: "Right" in white, "Plane" in #3fb950. The word "Right" carries dual meaning (correct + direction), which can be hinted at with a subtle right-pointing element — either a small chevron after the "t" or a baseline underline that sweeps right. Clean wordmark, no busy icon needed.

**Taglines**
1. "Correct angle. Correct result."
2. "Two angles. Zero guesswork."
3. "Your swing plane, finally right."

**App Store subtitle** (max 30 chars)
`Swing Plane AI, Two Angles`

**Easy Mode branding**
"RightMode" — clean, short, stays in family. In-app: "RightMode: point, record, improve."

**One-line elevator pitch**
RightPlane analyzes your swing from down-the-line and face-on, uses AI to detect faults by name, and gives you a score you can actually improve.

**Potential issues**
- "Right plane" is slightly awkward phonetically for some speakers — the two "r" sounds run together.
- Could be read as directional ("the right side") rather than correctness by non-golfers. Not a problem in golf context but limits crossover appeal.
- Stronger trademark potential than OnPlane since it's a compound word with less generic use.
- No major conflicts apparent, but "RightPlane" in aviation and flight simulation contexts exists — monitor for brand confusion in tech-adjacent spaces.

## TruePlane

**Logo concept**
Premium execution: "True" in a lighter weight, "Plane" in full-weight green (#3fb950). The period or a small dot between words (True•Plane) adds a structured, premium feel reminiscent of watchmakers or precision instruments. Logo icon optional — a clean swing arc rendered as a thin geometric line.

**Taglines**
1. "Precision for the serious golfer."
2. "See your swing. Know the truth."
3. "The honest read on your game."

**App Store subtitle** (max 30 chars)
`Precision Swing Analysis AI`

**Easy Mode branding**
"TrueSnap" — premium feel, implies quick and accurate capture. Alternatively "TrueMode" if internal consistency matters more than flair.

**One-line elevator pitch**
TruePlane gives you broadcast-quality swing analysis from your phone — two angles, AI-detected faults, and a score that tracks your actual improvement.

**Potential issues**
- "TruePlane" sounds aspirational but slightly generic in the "True[X]" tech brand space (TrueDepth, TrueTone, etc.) — risks feeling like an Apple sub-feature rather than a standalone product.
- The premium positioning implied by the name puts pricing pressure on the brand. At $79 CAD/year it works, but any perception of the product as unfinished undercuts the name promise.
- Trademark search recommended — "TruePlane" or "True Plane" may exist in aviation/manufacturing contexts.
- Excellent App Store discoverability: "true swing plane" is a real search term used by golfers.

## Caddie

**Logo concept**
Friendly, rounded sans-serif — not geometric, not sharp. The double-d gets the green accent or the "a" does if a single-letter highlight is preferred. A small bag or pin icon could work but risks being too cute. The brand wants warmth, not precision-instrument energy. White wordmark with green accent letter, dark background.

**Taglines**
1. "Your swing coach, always in your bag."
2. "Read the shot. Read the swing."
3. "AI in your corner. Bag on your back."

**App Store subtitle** (max 30 chars)
`AI Swing Coach in Your Pocket`

**Easy Mode branding**
"Quick Read" — fits the caddie metaphor (a caddie gives you a quick read on the shot). Clean and intuitive for non-technical users.

**One-line elevator pitch**
Caddie is your AI swing coach — film two angles, get instant fault detection, and walk away knowing exactly what to work on.

**Potential issues**
- High trademark risk. "Caddie" and "Caddy" are extremely common in golf apps, accessories, hardware, and SaaS products. "Caddie AI" and "Golf Caddie" variants exist in multiple app stores already.
- Not trademarkable as a standalone word in the golf category — descriptive term.
- Discoverability challenge: App Store search for "Caddie" returns generic results, not AI swing tools. Hard to own.
- Best-suited for a companion/coach app rather than a precision swing analysis tool — the name might undersell the technical depth of the product.

## Piped

**Logo concept**
Bold, short, punchy — the name earns an equally confident visual. All caps: "PIPED". The "I" gets the green accent (#3fb950), styled as a vertical swing plane line — clean and conceptually on-point. No icon needed. The brevity of the name is the brand statement.

**Taglines**
1. "Hit it pure. Every time."
2. "Flush your faults. Flush your shots."
3. "The app for when close isn't close enough."

**App Store subtitle** (max 30 chars)
`AI Golf Swing Fault Detector`

**Easy Mode branding**
"Pure Mode" — golf slang for a perfectly struck shot. Tight alignment with the brand's slang-forward energy. In-app: "Pure Mode: two clips, instant result."

**One-line elevator pitch**
Piped uses AI pose detection to analyze your swing from two angles, identify what's off, and show you the path to hitting it pure every time.

**Potential issues**
- Very low brand-name discoverability. "Piped" has no existing golf SEO footprint — you'd be building search intent from zero.
- Non-golfers won't understand the name at all. Works brilliantly for the target audience, nearly meaningless outside it.
- Short names are increasingly trademarked — "Piped" exists as a software product in SaaS/data pipeline categories (Pipedrive, Piped.com). Trademark clearance required before investment in this name.
- Risk: feels niche and insider-coded, which could be a feature (community credibility) or a bug (limited download appeal in casual golfers).
