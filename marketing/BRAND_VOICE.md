# Brand Voice — Golf Swing AI

This document defines how the product communicates across every surface: UI copy, notifications, App Store text, marketing, and error states. It is a working reference for writing decisions, not a mood board. Every rule here exists because a specific wrong pattern was worth naming.

---

## The Three Adjectives

**Precise**
We name things exactly. Not "something's off with your backswing" — "your shoulder tilt exceeds 12 degrees at P3." Precision is what separates this product from a coach's feelings and a YouTube tip. The writing earns trust by being specific where it can be and honest about uncertainty where it can't.

**Direct**
We don't hedge. We don't soften useful information into mush. A fault is a fault. A score is a score. Users came here because they want the real read, not reassurance. Directness doesn't mean cold — it means we respect the user enough to tell them what we actually see.

**Encouraging without being patronizing**
Progress is the frame for everything, but we don't fake optimism. "Your swing has potential" is filler. "Your tempo ratio improved 18% since last week" is the same sentiment, done honestly. We celebrate real movement in the right direction. We don't celebrate showing up.

---

## How We Talk About Faults

Faults are diagnostic information, not verdicts. The user's swing produced a fault; the fault doesn't define the user. We describe faults the way a good sports medicine doctor describes an injury: matter-of-fact about what it is, forward-looking about what addresses it.

**We don't punish. We diagnose and point forward.**

| Wrong phrasing (punishing/vague) | Our phrasing (precise/forward) |
|---|---|
| "Your backswing is broken." | "Early extension detected at impact — your hips are moving toward the ball before contact." |
| "You're doing a lot wrong here." | "We found two faults this session: reverse pivot and head movement. Let's start with reverse pivot — it's driving the other one." |
| "That swing has major problems." | "Shoulder tilt flagged at P4. This is common when the trail elbow drops early — here's where it shows up in your footage." |

**Principle:** Lead with the fault name, describe what the body is actually doing, and wherever possible, give the fault a mechanical cause rather than leaving it as an isolated criticism.

## How We Talk About Scores

The swing score is a measurement, not a grade. There's no "bad" score — there's your score today and where it can go. A 42 is data. A 91 is also data. The goal is movement in the right direction over time.

**We don't judge. We track.**

| Wrong phrasing (judgmental) | Our phrasing (progress-oriented) |
|---|---|
| "Poor score. You need a lot of work." | "Score: 44. Two active faults are pulling this down — address the reverse pivot first and you'll see that number move." |
| "You didn't improve this session." | "Score held steady at 61. No regression — and your tempo ratio improved. The mechanics are stabilizing." |
| "You scored 78 — not bad, but there's still a lot to fix." | "Score: 78. That's your best this month. Head movement is the last major fault standing — get that fixed and you're looking at 85+." |

**Principle:** Every score mention should include a next step, a trend note, or a specific thing to watch. A number alone is noise. A number with context is a coaching signal.

## Push Notification Copy

These are written for a user who is mid-improvement cycle — someone who has filmed swings before and is checking in. Notifications should feel like a message from someone who has actually watched their footage, not a generic reminder bot.

1. "You fixed your head movement. Went from a flagged fault to clean across 3 sessions. That's real."
2. "Reverse pivot is still showing up at the top of your backswing. 4 sessions in a row. Worth a focused session on this one."
3. "Your tempo ratio is the best it's been in 6 weeks. Whatever you changed in that last session — do it again."
4. "It's been 9 days since your last swing. Your shoulder tilt was trending in the right direction. Pick it up before that loses ground."
5. "New session ready to analyze. Last time: score 67, early extension flagged. Let's see where you're at."

## Empty State Copy

Empty states are underwritten in most apps. They're a missed coaching moment. Ours should feel like a good caddie: calm, useful, zero judgment.

**1. No swings yet**

> Your swing analysis starts here.
>
> Film your first swing from two angles — down-the-line and face-on — and get a score, fault report, and tempo breakdown in under a minute.
>
> [Analyze My First Swing]

*Tone note:* No "Welcome!" energy. No "You haven't recorded anything yet." Just tell them what's waiting and how to get it.

**2. No faults found — clean swing**

> Clean session.
>
> No faults flagged this swing. Your score reflects solid mechanics across all four fault categories: shoulder tilt, reverse pivot, head movement, and early extension.
>
> Tempo ratio: [X.X]. Keep filming — a clean session is worth tracking.

*Tone note:* Don't oversell it. "Clean session" is matter-of-fact and accurate. We add the tempo data because a fault-free swing is still worth measurement. Avoid "Perfect!" or "Amazing!" — those feel cheap against a product that prides itself on precision.

**3. Camera not positioned correctly**

> We couldn't get a clean read on your setup angle.
>
> For down-the-line: stand directly behind the ball, camera at hip height, shaft pointing at the lens.
> For face-on: stand directly in front, same height, face the target.
>
> [See Setup Guide]

*Tone note:* No apology. No "Oops!" No tech-speak about detection failure. Tell them what was wrong and exactly how to fix it. The setup guide link should go to a short visual reference, not a wall of text.

## Words We Use vs. Words We Avoid

| We Use | We Avoid |
|---|---|
| Fault | Error, mistake, flaw, problem |
| Flagged | Detected wrong, broken, bad |
| Session | Workout, practice round (too vague) |
| Analyze | Grade, judge, evaluate |
| Score | Rating (implies opinion, not measurement) |
| Improve | Fix (implies something's broken about the person) |
| Trend | Progress (too congratulatory before it's earned) |
| Phase | Stage, step (phase is technical and accurate) |
| Tempo ratio | Swing speed (vague, conflates different measurements) |
| Clean | Perfect, flawless (sets unearnable expectations) |
| Film / Record | Capture, shoot (film is action-oriented and clear) |
| Down-the-line | Behind (imprecise), DTL (use on second reference only) |
| Your swing | The swing (possessive keeps the user connected to the data) |
| Let's | You should, you need to (collaborative framing) |

## Additional Voice Principles

**We never congratulate effort. We recognize results.**
"Great job recording a swing!" is not a thing we say. "Your consistency score improved" is.

**We abbreviate for returning users, explain for new ones.**
First-time empty states explain DTL and Face-On. After three sessions, we can use the shorthand. The product learns; so should the copy.

**Technical terms earn their keep.**
Tempo ratio, reverse pivot, early extension, P3/P4 (swing phases) — these are used because they are precise, not because they sound impressive. If a simpler term carries the same meaning without losing accuracy, use it.

**The product is direct. The brand is not loud.**
No exclamation points in diagnostic copy. No "Wow!" or "Incredible!" reactions to score improvements. Confidence is quiet. The number speaks. The copy frames it.
