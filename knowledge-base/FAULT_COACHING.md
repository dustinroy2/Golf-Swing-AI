# Fault Coaching Reference

This file documents the coaching content for each detectable swing fault in the Shank app.
The data lives in `src/data/faultCoaching.ts` and is consumed by `CauseChainScreen` and `FixScreen`.

---

## Fault: Shoulder Tilt at Address

**Phase:** Setup
**Phase Explainer:** Your starting position before the club moves. Everything cascades from here.

**Consequence:**
Pre-tilted shoulders rotate on a tilted axis. The path swings outside-in and the face opens — a slice or a pull is almost guaranteed.

**Correct Position:**
Shoulders sit parallel to the ground and square to the target line. Spine angle is neutral — slight forward tilt, no lateral lean.

**Shot Shapes:** Push, Pull

**Drill: Mirror Shoulder Check**
1. Stand in front of a full-length mirror in your address position.
2. Place a club shaft across your shoulders and look at the angle.
3. Adjust until the shaft is level — memorize that feeling.
4. Hit 20 balls with that setup feeling before each shot.

**Fix Quote:** "Fix the setup and half your problems disappear before the club moves."

**Annotated Keypoints:**
- Left Shoulder (kp 5) — High, needs to be level
- Right Shoulder (kp 6) — Low, creating tilt

## Fault: Reverse Pivot

**Phase:** Top of Backswing
**Phase Explainer:** The highest point of your backswing, just before you start the downswing. This is where your body stores power.

**Consequence:**
Forces a compensating lunge toward the ball at impact. The path goes steeply down-and-out, the face opens — slices, shanks, and topped shots.

**Correct Position:**
70–80% of pressure on trail foot at the top. Head stays behind the ball. Lead shoulder under the chin. Hips rotate 45°, shoulders 90°.

**Shot Shapes:** Slice, Push, Topped

**Drill: Wall Drill**
1. Stand with your trail hip just touching a wall or golf bag.
2. Grip a club and take your address position — trail hip barely makes contact.
3. Backswing: feel your trail hip press into the wall as weight loads.
4. If your hip pulls away from the wall, you are reverse-pivoting.
5. Hit 10 balls with the wall feedback, then step away and swing free.

**Fix Quote:** "Load into the wall, unload at the ball. Power comes from the ground, not a lunge."

**Annotated Keypoints:**
- Lead Shoulder (kp 5) — Shifted toward target, should load back
- Lead Hip (kp 11) — Weight forward at top

## Fault: Head Up at Impact

**Phase:** Moment of Impact
**Phase Explainer:** The split second when the club face meets the ball. Every fault upstream shows up here.

**Consequence:**
Lifting the head early shortens the arc and opens the face at contact. The ball rockets thin, slices right, or catches the hosel.

**Correct Position:**
Eyes on ball position until after contact. Head stays level — or even slightly lower — through the strike zone. "See the ground long after the ball is gone."

**Shot Shapes:** Thin, Shank, Slice

**Drill: Tee Drill**
1. Place a tee in the ground at your ball position (no ball needed).
2. Take your normal swing — try to keep your eyes on the tee after impact.
3. The goal: see the tee still there when your arms are fully extended.
4. "Watch the tee disappear" — not "look up to see where it went."

**Fix Quote:** "Stay down an extra half-second and the ball takes care of itself."

**Annotated Keypoints:**
- Head/Nose (kp 0) — Rising before contact, stay level

## Fault: Early Extension

**Phase:** Moment of Impact
**Phase Explainer:** The split second when the club face meets the ball. Every fault upstream shows up here.

**Consequence:**
Hips thrust toward the ball push the club off-plane. The hosel arrives at the ball before the face — that is the shank. Also causes chunks and thins.

**Correct Position:**
Hips open 30–40° but stay back. Trail heel lifts naturally. Hands lead the face at contact with forward shaft lean. Spine angle held from address.

**Shot Shapes:** Shank, Chunk, Thin

**Drill: Glute Wall Drill**
1. Set up with your trail glute touching a wall or bag — barely making contact.
2. Address the ball normally — maintain that light glute contact.
3. Swing through impact: glute must stay on the wall. Rotate, don't slide.
4. If your glutes leave the wall toward the ball, you are early extending.
5. Do this for 15 minutes. The groove will transfer to the course.

**Fix Quote:** "Fix the hip thrust and the shank disappears. The hosel can't reach the ball if the hips rotate instead of lunge."

**Annotated Keypoints:**
- Lead Hip (kp 11) — Lunging toward ball, rotate don't slide
- Trail Hip (kp 12) — Should be rotating open, not thrusting
